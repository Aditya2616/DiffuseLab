noise schedule

How fast the signal disappears

The schedule fixes βt, the noise added at each of the 1000 forward steps, and so ᾱt, how much of the original survives at step t. The linear schedule from the first DDPM paper loses most of the signal early; the cosine schedule from Improved DDPM spends more steps in the middle of the range. Those curves are computed in your browser from the formulas and checked against the Python code in the repo.

the forward process

Noising is a closed form

Training never simulates the 1000 forward steps. It jumps straight to any t: scale the image by √ᾱt, add noise scaled by √(1−ᾱt). Left and middle are the two terms; the right panel is their sum, computed live here.

2-D laboratory

The reverse process as a point cloud

Images hide the mechanism, so here it is in two dimensions, computed exactly in your browser. No network is trained: for a finite set of data points the ideal noise predictor has a closed form, so every arrow and every step below is the real algorithm with the real optimal denoiser. Because that denoiser knows the exact training set, the cloud lands on the training points themselves; that is memorisation, shown honestly.
